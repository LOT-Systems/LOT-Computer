## THE ROGUE TERMINAL — THEME OVERVIEW

The roguelike genre names something true about practice: every day is a new run.
You do not carry your yesterday's energy into today — you carry only the knowledge
of what killed you last time. The dungeon is procedurally generated. No two days
have the same floor configuration. You cannot memorize your way through; you can
only build the skills that work on any floor.

Permadeath is not the game's cruelty — it is its wisdom. Because every run ends,
every run counts. There is no low-stakes day. The journal entry you write on a
Tuesday is not practice for some more important Friday — it is the run. The
class you choose (healer, rogue, mage, warrior) is not permanent — but knowing
which one you are playing today makes you better at it.

The vocabulary of roguelikes is the vocabulary of serious self-care: going deeper,
mapping the floor, finding the loot in unexpected rooms, surviving the boss,
naming the final challenge. This is what all self-care practitioners actually do.
The terminal already knows this.

## FLAVOR TEXT — THE ROGUE TERMINAL

> *"The rogue does not attack the dungeon — the rogue learns the dungeon. Each floor
> teaches the next. Each death teaches the next run. The practice is not about
> winning once — it is about understanding the system well enough that winning becomes
> possible." — LOT Systems riff on roguelike game design philosophy.*

> *"Procedural generation is not randomness — it is the same principles producing
> infinite variations. Your days are procedurally generated. The floor is different
> every time. Your responses are the character build that persists across all runs."
> — LOT Systems internal notes, v33 session.*

> *"It is pitch dark. You are likely to be eaten by a grue." — Zork, Infocom, 1980.
> The grue lives in the dark. The journal is the torch. Keep writing.*

> *"Elbereth." — NetHack, 1987. The one word that makes monsters flee. Know your
> Elbereth. Write it when the room gets crowded. The dungeon respects it.*

> *"Thou art the Avatar." — Ultima IV, 1985. Richard Garriott built a game where
> you could not win by defeating an enemy — only by mastering eight virtues.
> The earliest recorded design of self-care as gameplay.*

> *"Every run is a new character. The player is the same." — LOT Systems, v33.*

- `stats.streak` — integer, current consecutive daily check-in streak
