## THE STOIC TERMINAL — THEME OVERVIEW

The Stoics ran philosophy like an operating system: load the principles at boot,
run the virtues as processes, accept the kernel panic, and recover with reason.
Marcus Aurelius wrote Meditations as private debug logs. Epictetus published a
handbook — a 53-paragraph README for the examined life. Seneca sent his letters
as API documentation to a friend.

The vocabulary these thinkers released — amor fati, memento mori, ataraxia,
eudaimonia — is not academic. It is the self-care vocabulary of people who had
to operate under extreme conditions: a slave, an emperor, a man condemned to die.
They ran the terminal in the hardest environments ever attempted.

Word Turn v23 makes this vocabulary into LOT triggers. When you write "amor fati"
in your journal, you are not citing a philosopher — you are running a command.
When you write "present moment", the system recognizes the input.

## FLAVOR TEXT — THE STOIC TERMINAL

> *"You have power over your mind, not outside events. Realize this, and you will
> find strength." — Marcus Aurelius, Meditations. The terminal cannot control its
> environment. It can only control how it processes the input.*

> *"It is not things that disturb us, but our opinions about things." — Epictetus,
> Enchiridion. The emotion is not the data. The opinion is a subroutine. It can
> be rewritten.*

> *"Omnia aliena sunt, tempus tantum nostrum est." — Seneca. Everything else belongs
> to others. Time alone is ours. Every check-in is a reclamation of what is actually
> yours.*

> *"Amor fati — that one wants nothing to be different, not forward, not backward,
> not in all eternity. Not merely bear what is necessary, still less conceal it—
> all idealism is mendaciousness in the face of what is necessary — but love it."
> — Nietzsche. The Stoic root.*

> *"No man is free who is not master of himself." — Epictetus. The daily practice
> is the mastery protocol. Running it is the freedom.*

### Wire-up guide for runJournalEasterEggs() / runCheckInEasterEggs()

Add these calls to the appropriate runners:
- Journal saves: `checkStoicSession`, `checkMeditationsEntry`
- Check-in events: `checkEveningReview`
