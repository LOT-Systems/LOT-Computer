## THE STOIC TERMINAL — THEME OVERVIEW

Stoicism is the original RPG of the self: a discipline system where the character
stat that matters is virtue, the enemies are passion and distraction, and the
final boss is the self. Marcus Aurelius spent twenty years writing down the rules
of this game — and he was the most powerful man in the Roman world at the time.

The Stoic Terminal is the LOT metaphor for this: the terminal (the self, the
practice, the journal) running clean, running with discipline, running in accordance
with reason. The vocabulary of Stoicism is also a vocabulary of self-care that
predates the category by two thousand years.

## FLAVOR TEXT — THE STOIC TERMINAL

> *"You have power over your mind — not outside events. Realize this, and you will find
> strength." — Marcus Aurelius, Meditations. Every journal entry is a re-assertion of
> this principle. You cannot control the world. You can run the terminal clean.*

> *"We suffer more in imagination than in reality." — Seneca. Negative visualization is
> the cure: imagine the worst so clearly that the actual, whatever it is, arrives lighter.
> The Stoic journals not to escape difficulty — but to pre-process it.*

> *"First say to yourself what you would be; and then do what you have to do." —
> Epictetus. The terminal has a boot sequence. What you would be is the kernel.
> Every check-in executes it.*

> *"The impediment to action advances action. What stands in the way becomes the way."
> — Marcus Aurelius. Amor fati, technically. The obstacle as material. The terminal
> uses resistance as input. This is not philosophy — it is engineering.*

> *"It is not that I'm so smart. But I stay with the questions much longer." — attributed
> to Einstein, but the Stoics lived it. Zeno of Citium founded a school in a public
> porch (stoa) after losing his cargo in a shipwreck. The wreck was the lesson.
> The porch was the answer. The terminal boots from loss.*
