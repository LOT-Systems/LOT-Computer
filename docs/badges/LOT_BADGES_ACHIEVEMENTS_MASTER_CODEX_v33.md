## THE DUNGEON MASTER — THEME OVERVIEW

The tabletop RPG is the original self-care technology.

Long before apps, wearables, and wellness trackers, people gathered around a table
with dice, paper, and character sheets — and built worlds together. Dungeons &
Dragons, first published in 1974 by Gary Gygax, gave people a vocabulary for
courage, for setbacks, for growth, for rest, for community, and for facing the
final boss. Every session was a practice of showing up, of describing what your
character does, of narrating the self through adventure.

The vocabulary of the TTRPG is, at its core, the vocabulary of intentional living:
- Long rest: recovery is not optional; it is mechanics
- Initiative: who acts first is who shows up
- Experience points: growth is measured and visible
- The dungeon master: someone holds the story so the players can be in it
- Natural 20: perfection is random, but you can only roll if you showed up

These are not metaphors. They are systems that encode self-care.

## FLAVOR TEXT — THE DUNGEON MASTER

> *"The purpose of the game is for everyone to have fun." — Gary Gygax. The dungeon
> master builds the world. The players live in it. In self-care: you are both.*

> *"Natural 20 — it happens. It means you were there when it landed." — table wisdom.
> You can't roll nat twenty if you don't roll. Every check-in is rolling the dice.*

> *"A paladin falls not from fighting evil — but from refusing to see the good." —
> table wisdom. Character class is not destiny. It is a starting position.*

> *"The dungeon master never loses — they just watch what the players discover." —
> table wisdom. You are running this campaign. The discovery is the point.*

> *"Long rest. All spell slots restored. HP fully recovered. This is how it works in
> the game. This is also how it works in real life." — the mechanics are honest.*
