## THE ROLEPLAYER — THEME OVERVIEW

The tabletop roleplaying game is the oldest form of collaborative storytelling with
mechanics. Dungeons & Dragons was published in 1974 — a boxed set of rules that turned
imagination into a structured language for adventure. Hit points, saving throws, spell
slots: these are not game abstractions. They are the vocabulary of survival, recovery,
and power management that every serious practitioner of self-care already uses.

You do not have infinite spell slots. You need a long rest. The saving throw is the
moment between the hit and the damage. The party rally is why you do not practice alone.
Natural 20 is the moment everything goes perfectly right. The character sheet is a map
of who you are right now.

This is not metaphor. This is the vocabulary of the practice named correctly.

## FLAVOR TEXT — THE ROLEPLAYER

> *"The whole point of the game is the story." — Gary Gygax. Every journal entry
> is a session log. Every check-in is a roll. The dungeon is real. The loot is real.
> The rest at the inn after a hard encounter is what makes the next encounter possible.*

> *"I never lost the sense of wonder about the game." — Gary Gygax. Born July 27, 1938.
> He gave the world a language for adventure: initiative, saving throw, hit points,
> long rest. These are not fantasy. They are the structure of survival.*

> *"Roll for initiative." The most important sentence in tabletop gaming. It asks: who
> acts first? The practice asks the same. The check-in is rolling for initiative in
> your own story. You go first.*

> *"The dungeon master is the first author of every session." — Gygax. The DM does not
> win. The DM makes the world worth playing in. That is what self-care does: it makes
> the world worth playing in.*

> *"Planescape's Sigil is the City of Doors — every faction believes something different
> about the planes, and the Lady of Pain does not explain herself." — Tony DiTerlizzi.
> The self is the City of Doors. Every door leads somewhere. The practice is what lets
> you walk through them.*
