## THE TERMINAL KINGDOM — THEME OVERVIEW

The Terminal Kingdom maps the vocabulary of RPG/dungeon/arcade culture onto the
practice of self-care. Every serious practitioner of any discipline knows these
patterns: the respawn when you fail, the checkpoint when you need to save your
progress, the dungeon run that demands sustained effort, the boss battle that
mirrors your deepest resistance.

The language of games is not frivolous. It is a vocabulary for growth under
pressure — one that millions of people already speak fluently. The Terminal Kingdom
makes that vocabulary explicit: *the dungeon is the practice, the XP is real,
and the boss you face is always some version of yourself.*

The three secret bosses — Zelda, Konami, Mordor — are not random. They are:

- **Zelda Signal**: the first NPC who ever offered help
- **Konami Code**: the hidden room behind the hidden door
- **Mordor Path**: the journey that cannot be shortcut

These are the three shapes that all difficult practices take.

## DESIGN DECISIONS

| Decision | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| `guild_signal` avoids `party` ambiguity | "party" appears in too many non-RPG contexts; guild+fellowship are unambiguous |
| `tavern_rest` omits bare "inn" | "inn" too common; "tavern" or "resting place" = clear RPG vocabulary |
| `dungeon_run` at 21 days | 3×7 — triple the week. The number carries weight without being too rare |
| `respawn_return` at 14 days | Shorter than `ghost_protocol` (7d gap + return) — the game uses the word |
| `turing_day` at EPIC | Turing is not a game character, he is the architect of all games |
| `dungeon_day` Jan 26 | D&D booklet published January 26, 1974 — the opening of the dungeon |
| `mordor_path` at MYTHIC | The hardest badge — requires knowing the exact phrase |
| `dungeon_elder` at 1,000 days | The first four-digit milestone — a true endgame achievement |
| `tome_complete` at 200k words | A published novel is ~80k; 200k is two novels and a half |
